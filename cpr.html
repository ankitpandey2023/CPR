Rhythm Identification on the ED Monitor

SHOCKABLE RHYTHMS (Defibrillation Indicated)

VF & Pulseless VT

Ventricular Fibrillation (chaotic wave) and Pulseless Ventricular Tachycardia (monomorphic wide complex). Prepare defibrillator immediately.

NON-SHOCKABLE RHYTHMS (Manual CPR & Epi Only)

Asystole & PEA

Asystole (flatline) and Pulseless Electrical Activity (organized ECG rhythm without a palpable mechanical pulse). Defibrillation is contraindicated.

Physiology: Coronary Perfusion Pressure (CPP)

Targeting Oxygenation of the Myocardium

The main physiological goal of chest compressions is to achieve a Coronary Perfusion Pressure (CPP) > 15 mmHg (calculated as diastolic aortic pressure minus right atrial pressure).

CPP Building Phase

It takes 10 to 15 continuous, deep compressions to build a protective CPP. Any pause instantly drops this pressure to zero.

ED Bed Preparation & Scene Optimization

Setting Up for Success

An ED resuscitation bed requires immediate adjustment before beginning chest compressions. Resuscitating on a soft mattress significantly limits cardiac output.

  • **Deploy CPR Board:** Instantly slide the rigid cardiac backboard under the patient's thorax.
  • **Adjust Bed Height:** Lower the bed so the compressor's hips are above their shoulders, maximizing leverage.
  • **Remove Headboard:** Quickly unlatch the headboard of the bed to allow easy access for airway placement.

Clinical Patient Assessment

Advanced Responsive Testing

For patients in the ED, assessment must bypass bystander verbal methods. Rapidly identify circulatory arrest with clinical markers:

"No palpable carotid pulse & agonal respirations present. Monitor shows Ventricular Fibrillation. Activate Code Blue!"

Immediately connect monitoring leads, check pupillary response, and prep the defibrillator pad sites on the patient's chest.

Critical Diagnostic Rule

Do not spend more than 10 seconds checking pulse or breathing. If in doubt, begin chest compressions immediately. Unnecessary pauses damage tissue survival.

Proceed to Immediate Code Allocation.

Capnography & ETCO2 Tracking

End-Tidal CO2 (ETCO2) Monitoring

Waveform capnography is the gold standard in the ED to monitor the effectiveness of chest compressions and confirm advanced airway placement.

Low ETCO2 (< 10 mmHg)

Indicates poor chest compression quality. Re-evaluate compressor's depth, posture, or speed immediately.

Spike in ETCO2 (> 35 mmHg)

A sudden, sustained spike indicates **Return of Spontaneous Circulation (ROSC)**.

Capnography Waveforms

Continuous monitor tracking tells the resuscitation team whether they are successfully circulating blood. Do not perform pulse checks unless ETCO2 trends or ECG waveform changes indicate ROSC.

Mechanics of High-Quality Resuscitation

Advanced Manual Blood Pump

Resuscitation requires proper technique to maintain perfusion pressure in a clinical bed setting.

Utilize Step Stools

Keep standard medical step stools next to all resuscitation beds to ensure correct vertical posture.

Resuscitation Checklist

  • Place patient flat on a backboard
  • Position shoulders directly over the patient's sternum
  • Swap compressors every 2 minutes (5 cycles) to prevent fatigue

Resuscitation Posture in the ED

Vertical Alignment & Gravity Leverage

For nursing staff, performing compressions on elevated ED beds is exhausting. Standing on a step stool allows you to use your upper body weight, preserving your stamina and ensuring consistent chest depth.

Inadequate compression depth fails to empty cardiac ventricles. Always use a step stool for optimal force delivery.

Compression Depth, Rate & Metronome

Critical Clinical Metrics

1

Target Depth: 2.0 to 2.4 inches (5–6 cm)

Ensure the chest fully recoils between compressions. Leaning on the chest blocks venous return and coronary perfusion.

2

Target Rate: 100 to 120 compressions per minute

Consistent, fast compressions are needed to maintain cerebral oxygenation.

AHA Practice Metronome
110 BPM

Practice compression timing along with this beat.

Professional Airway Management

Oxygenation & Advanced Devices

For ED patients, airway management escalates rapidly from simple bag-valve-mask (BVM) ventilation to advanced airway placement.

Double C-E Technique for BVM

Use a two-provider BVM technique: one provider uses both hands to seal the mask (forming 'C' and 'E' shapes), while the other squeezes the bag. This ensures an optimal seal and prevents gastric inflation.

Advanced Airway Ventilation Rate

Once an endotracheal tube or supraglottic device (such as an LMA) is placed, do not stop compressions. Deliver **1 breath every 6 seconds (10 breaths per minute)** continuously.

Over-Ventilation Warning: Excessive ventilation increases pressure in the chest, reducing venous blood return to the heart. Keep breaths controlled and brief (1 second).
Charak Hospital ED • Airway Protocols

CPR Cycles: BLS vs. Advanced Airway

Resuscitation Coordination

The resuscitation cycle is determined by whether an advanced airway is in place. Minimizing pauses remains the top clinical priority.

30 : 2 Standard BLS Cycle

Squeeze BVM during brief pauses after 30 compressions.

10 / Min Continuous Compressions

Uninterrupted chest compressions with asynchronous ventilations.

When to Check Rhythms:

  • Every 2 minutes (or after 5 cycles of 30:2)
  • Limit rhythm checks to under 10 seconds
  • Pre-charge the defibrillator during compressions to minimize delay

    Crash Cart & Manual Defibrillation

    Advanced Energy Delivery

    Unlike public-access AEDs, ED nursing staff operate manual biphasic/monophasic defibrillators. This allows for immediate energy customization and synchronized cardioversion.

    Biphasic Defibrillation Energy

    Initial dose: **120J to 200J** (depending on manufacturer). If unknown, charge to maximum energy (**200J**).

    Monophasic Defibrillation

    If your emergency cart uses an older monophasic defibrillator, deliver shocks at **360J** for all attempts.

    Child Advanced Care (1 Year to Puberty)

    Mechanics for Children

    Children have highly compliant chest cages. The compression technique must be scaled to prevent structural injury while maintaining cardiac output.

    1 or 2 Hands: Use 1 hand for smaller children, and 2 hands for older, larger pediatric patients.
    Depth Limit: Press down approximately 2 inches (5 cm).

    Two-Rescuer Healthcare Ratio:

    Unlike the default bystander 30:2 ratio, clinical teams in the ED must use a specialized ratio:

    2-Provider Team Ratio: 15 : 2 Cycle

    15 compressions followed by 2 breaths to optimize oxygenation and prevent hypoxemic arrest.

    Charak Hospital ED • Pediatric Care

    Infant Advanced Resuscitation (Under 1 Year)

    Two Thumb-Encircling Hands Technique

    For two-rescuer healthcare teams, the two-thumb technique is preferred. This method provides more consistent depth and higher coronary perfusion pressure compared to the two-finger technique.

    PALS Target Depth: Press down 1.5 inches (4 cm).
    Pulse Check Location: Check the brachial artery inside the upper arm.

    Advanced Cardiac Pharmacotherapy

    Code Medication Management

    For ED nurses, preparing and administering drugs during a Code Blue must follow a strict, systematic schedule.

    Epinephrine Admin Protocol

    Dose: **1mg (1:10,000 solution) IV/IO every 3 to 5 minutes**. Follow each dose with a **20 mL saline flush** and elevate the extremity to ensure rapid drug entry.

    Amiodarone (Shockable Rhythms Only)

    Indication: Refractory VF/pVT after the 3rd shock. Initial dose: **300mg IV/IO bolus**. A second dose of **150mg** can be given after the 5th shock if the rhythm persists.

    Lidocaine Alternative: If Amiodarone is unavailable, administer Lidocaine: initial dose **1 to 1.5 mg/kg IV/IO**, followed by **0.5 to 0.75 mg/kg** for subsequent doses.
    Charak Hospital ED • Pharmacotherapy

    Identifying Reversible Causes: 5 H's & 5 T's

    The 5 H's

    • **1. Hypovolemia:** (Administer rapid IV fluids or blood products)
    • **2. Hypoxia:** (Optimize ventilation and confirm airway oxygen supply)
    • **3. Hydrogen Ion (Acidosis):** (Improve ventilation; consider Sodium Bicarb)
    • **4. Hypo / Hyperkalemia:** (Check lab results; manage potassium levels)
    • **5. Hypothermia:** (Deploy active rewarming blankets)

    The 5 T's

    • **1. Tension Pneumothorax:** (Assist with needle decompression)
    • **2. Tamponade (Cardiac):** (Prepare for emergency pericardiocentesis)
    • **3. Toxins (Drug Overdose):** (Administer targeted reversal agents/antidotes)
    • **4. Thrombosis (Pulmonary):** (Consider emergency fibrinolytic therapy)
    • **5. Thrombosis (Coronary):** (Prepare for immediate transfer to Cath Lab)

    Manual Defibrillation Procedure

    Defibrillation Checklist

    Defibrillation must follow a highly structured, strict safety protocol.

    1
    Charge Defibrillator: Charge the device *while* chest compressions are ongoing to minimize pauses.
    2
    Visual Safety Check: Check the patient and bed. Confirm that the oxygen supply is directed away from the bed. Loudly call: **"All Clear!"**

    Code Documentation & the Scribe Role

    Accurate Time Tracking

    The Code Scribe plays a critical role in tracking the timeline of the resuscitation. This ensures that interventions are delivered on time and provides an accurate record for post-code evaluation.

    **1. Epi Timer:** Track the exact time of epinephrine doses. Prompt the team leader at the 3-minute mark: *"Geetanjali, 3 minutes have passed since the last Epinephrine dose."*

    **2. Defib Logger:** Record the exact energy level of each shock (e.g., 200J) and the patient's resulting cardiac rhythm.

    Critical Information to Document:

    • Time CPR was initiated
    • Time and results of all rhythm checks
    • ETCO2 levels at 2-minute intervals
    • Advanced airway confirmation times
    Charak Hospital ED • Code Documentation

    Post-Cardiac Arrest Care (ROSC)

    Critical Nursing Priorities

    Once ROSC is achieved, the patient remains highly unstable. Immediate, structured post-arrest care is needed to protect cerebral function.

    **1. Airway & Oxygenation:** Maintain oxygen saturation between **92% and 98%**. Avoid hyperoxia, which can worsen reperfusion injury.

    **2. Hemodynamic Targets:** Maintain a **Systolic Blood Pressure > 90 mmHg** and a **Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) > 65 mmHg**. Prepare vasopressor infusions as ordered.

    Targeted Temperature Management:

    For patients who remain unresponsive after ROSC, assist with initiating **Targeted Temperature Management (TTM)**.

    Cooling Parameters: Target temperature: **32°C to 36°C** maintained for at least **24 hours** to protect brain tissue.
